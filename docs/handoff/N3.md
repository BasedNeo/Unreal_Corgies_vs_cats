## 1. Thin beams in the nav grid (`src/sim/ai/nav.ts`)

**Cause (F1 §7, G4b §7), measured.** The grid probe is a capsule at the cell **centre**, from 0.45 m over the ground. A
hedgehog is three 0.2 m angle-iron beams pitched 35°, running from the ground to 1.4 m. The probe misses them in two
ways:
- where a beam crosses a cell away from its centre (a capsule of radius 0.42 m at the centre of a 1 m cell);
- where a beam runs low, under the probe's 0.45 m floor. A pet cannot step over it cleanly (autostep needs a 0.2 m wide
  top, exactly a beam's width), so it wedges against the beam or climbs onto it.

On HEAD the six West Yard hedgehogs had 1-3 of their 5-9 beam cells closed (ASCII maps in my scratch, `h/dump.mts`).
Paths ran through the open ones.

**Fix.** A thin obstacle is:
- a box whose two smaller half extents are ≤ 0.2 m (`THIN_HALF`): a beam, a post or a picket; a fence board (2 of 3
  extents large) is not one;
- an upright cylinder of radius ≤ 0.2 m: a pole.

After the probe pass, `buildNavGrid` closes every cell whose body column a thin obstacle intersects. The column runs from
`THIN_FLOOR` (0.08 m) over the cell's ground to the probe's top (1.53 m).
- The test is exact: a separating-axis test (Ericson §4.4.1) of the prop's oriented box against the cell's column, and a
  circle-vs-square test for a pole.
- There is no inflation. A cell a beam only passes beside stays open. F2's one-cell walkway beside the Lot's brace keeps
  its premise (`ai-stuck-unhug` passes).
- It is analytic, over the few cells of each thin prop's AABB, once per grid build. No Rapier query, and no per-tick
  cost: the grid is cached per world, as before.
- The decks (N1 `buildDeckGrid`) and the per-sim blockers (kiosks, parked karts, destructibles) are unchanged.

## 3. A plane pilot in Base Assault (`src/sim/ai/tactics.ts`, the gate only)

**Why one word was not enough.** G4b's note said adding the mode to `PLANE_MODES` is one word. It is not:
`objectiveGoal` returned into `baseAssaultGoal` before it ever reached `planeGoal`. So the Base Assault branch now:
1. runs `baseAssaultGoal` first, so the team plan and the bot's role stay current;
2. then calls `planeGoal` when all of these hold:
   - the bot's role is **attack**: the team's surplus, never the carrier, a guard, an escort, a returner or a chaser;
   - its team has made its **first storm** (`baPushCount`, G4b's exported telemetry);
   - no human teammate is in trouble nearby, as in the other modes.

`planeGoal` overwrites the goal only when it returns true, and a pilot's goal is `'step'`, so the brain's ball run (goal
`'ball'` only) never drives it. The rest of B2b is unchanged: the lowest-id Overwatch or Skyraider, ≥ 60 % health, no
teammate flying, no human heading there, a route up. The Lot has no Rooftops and so no hangar: its Base Assault is
untouched (bit-identical, §2).

**Carriers never board.** There are four guards:
- G4a's vehicle system refuses a carrier a seat;
- `vehicleThink` drops a carrier's boarding before `hangarBoard`;
- `planeGoal` now returns false for a carrier (new);
- a carrier's role is `carry`, never `attack` (the new gate).

The test is a pilot on its way that steals the enemy ball. Within the 0.5 s re-plan it drops the hangar goal, runs the
ball home and never boards or sits. With both new guards removed, it keeps walking to the hangar (444 ticks). Either
guard alone holds, as intended.

**Why "after the first storm" (game-design-psychology: bots that look purposeful).** The first version flew from any
attacker. Both teams' pilots then left at kick-off to race for the neutral hangar, and the opening pushes went in one
bot short. A/B, bot-only 4v4 on the West Yard, 300 s, limit 99, the same nav in every arm (KNOWLEDGE §8 and §13: pooled
seeds, against a reverted copy):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No BA pilot (N3's nav only) | 6-15 | 5.10 | 49 % | 16.1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| No BA pilot (N3's nav only) | 1-20 | **5.00** | 52 % | 16.1 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
