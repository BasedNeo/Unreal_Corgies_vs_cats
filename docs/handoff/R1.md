# R1 handoff: the RC plane and the Rooftop Hangar (Wave 4)

**Status: done, ready to integrate.** The authoritative flight model, the plane systems (seats, bail-outs, the gun,
damage, crashes), the Rooftop Hangar, the client views, the chase-camera parameters and the lab are finished and
proven. **No shared contract was changed.** The lead has three small paste-ready edits (camera pitch follow in
`main.ts`, the pilot tilt in `EntityViews`, S1's prompt for the neutral hangar) and one optional contract
proposal (`EFlag.Stunned`).

The **Fetch Flyer** (corgi side) / **Pounce Plane** (cat side) is a foam-winged toy RC plane, 1.6 m wingspan, that a
pet rides on top of, holding a handlebar. It flies arcade-style with **mouse-aim**: the plane chases where the camera
looks, banking into turns. It has a squeaky machine gun, a tennis-ball-can booster, and it explodes when it hits
something hard.

## Design decisions

**One neutral Rooftop Hangar, no per-base hangars.** I decided by fun:
- The Rooftops sit near the line of equal distance between the bases (D3). A single plane there is a **power
  position both teams fight over**, like the rocket-launcher spawn in an arena shooter. It gives the Overwatch perches
  on the same roof a reason to be contested, and the climb routes a reason to be used.
- **The sky stays readable.** One plane at a time per map (25 s cooldown after it is destroyed) keeps planes special.
  With a plane per team from minute one, a team could harass the other base from the air before anyone has left
  spawn.
- **Runways.** A take-off needs about 7 m of clear run (the roof gives 18.7 m). Both bases are cluttered (the house deck,
  the shed and cat tree), and V1's runtime site search looks for a 2-disc kiosk + pad, not a runway.
- PvP still gets planes: the hangar is placed in `yard-skirmish`, `team-deathmatch`, `core-rush` and `adventure`. It is
  neutral, so the plane takes the colors of whoever vends it.
- `sim.state.vehicleConfig = { hangar: false }` turns it off (an adventure chapter that doesn't want it). Per-base
  hangars would be a content switch plus a runway-aware site search, if a human playtest asks for them.

**Mouse-aim steering.** The camera aim (`InputCmd.yaw/pitch`) *is* the flight command. The plane turns toward it at up
to 1.6 rad/s, banks by the coordinated-turn angle, and settles on the aim with no overshoot.
- **Readable:** the crosshair shows where you're going. The gun gimbals up to 8° toward the camera's aim point, so the
  crosshair also shows where the bullets go.
- **Latency-tolerant:** the camera answers the mouse instantly while the interpolated plane catches up. That matters
  because planes are interpolated, not predicted (like karts).
- W/S is the throttle. A/D is a rudder (extra yaw and bank) for keyboard or gamepad pilots. Sprint is the boost.

## Flight numbers (measured by the tests; all tunables in `VEHICLES.rc_plane`)
| | Everyone | Skyraider |
|---|---|---|
| Cruise (full throttle, level) | 20 m/s (about 2× a sprinting corgi) | 23 m/s |
| Boost (Sprint, 1.3 s, then a 4.5 s cooldown) | 27 m/s | 31 m/s, cooldown ×0.7 |
| 360° turn | 4.08 s, circle ⌀ 22 m, bank 51° | 3.30 s |
| Take-off run | 6.9 m in 1.5 s (lift-off at 10 m/s) | same |

Climbing, gliding and stalling:
- **Climb:** 8 m/s zoom climb for 2 s, bleeding speed to 14 m/s. Sustained, about 7.7 m/s at 13.7 m/s.
- **Powered dive:** up to 32 m/s.
- **Engine idle:** glides at 11.9 m/s forward and 1.8 m/s down (6.6:1).
- **Stall protection:** holding the nose up with no power mushes into a glide instead of stalling.
- **Stall:** below 7 m/s the nose drops (−20°) and the plane is flying again in about 0.3 s.

Landing and crashing:
- **Landing:** a touchdown slower than 4.5 m/s into the surface, wings within 40° of level and the nose not diving,
  on a surface flatter than 26°. The West Yard circuit test lands at 1.9 m/s.
- A landed plane with the engine idle stays down; it takes off again only under power.
- **Crash:** any impact faster than 9 m/s into a surface. Softer bumps bounce and cost 8 hp per m/s above 3 m/s.
  A 17° graze along a wall scrapes and damages the plane without exploding.

Flight envelope:
- **Hard ceiling:** 34 m (the fuselage never passes it). Over the last 5 m the nose is pushed down.
- **Flight box:** the yard inside its fences, shrunk by 5 m. Within 18 m of an edge the plane turns home by itself.
  The hard edge clamps without damage, so the invisible boundary walls above the fences are never touched.

Hull and gun:
- **Hull:** 140 hp, hit radius 1.0 m. Hitscan does ×0.8 damage, blasts full. Plane hits are **lag-compensated**
  through a 32-tick center history, like the rifles' character hits.
- **Gun:** 6 damage at 9 shots/s, falling off to 50 % at 45 m (65 m range). It overheats after about 18 shots (2 s),
  locks, and cools at 0.45/s until it is back under 30 % heat. Its hits on characters are lag-compensated.
- A full burst deals at most about 110 damage. A plane survives about 1.2 s of sustained squeaker-rifle fire.

Crash blast and ejection:
- **Crash blast:** through the combat lane's `explode()`: r 4.2 m, 50 damage, 12 m/s knockback.
- The pilot is thrown up (8 m/s) and back, and **stunned for 0.8 s**: no movement, no buttons.
- The pilot takes the blast's self-damage (25). The crash is credited to whoever shot the plane (last 10 s), else the
  pilot. After a bail-out it goes to the last pilot (last 10 s), so a pilot can bail out and aim the empty plane at
  something.

## Paste-ready lead edits
**1. `src/client/main.ts`, the camera** (the kart path is unchanged because its `pitchFollow` is 0). A plane's camera
eases its pitch toward the plane's own pitch when the mouse is idle. For planes the aim *is* the command, so this makes
a hands-off plane hold its attitude while the view settles behind it. It also brings the view back behind the nose for
gamepad and keyboard pilots.
```ts
import { damp } from '../shared/math';                       // add to the imports
// in the frame loop (replaces the one-line followYaw):
      if (!input.lookedRecently()) {
        input.yaw = followYaw(input.yaw, c.yaw, c.followRate, dt);                    // swing behind the vehicle
        if (c.pitchFollow > 0) input.pitch = damp(input.pitch, c.pitch, c.pitchFollow, dt); // R1: planes settle behind the nose
      }
```
Nothing else changes in `main.ts`:
- `createVehicleViews` already draws planes and the hangar.
- `mountedVehicle` / `vehicleCameraFor` already return the plane and its camera. It uses a 5.8 → 8.4 m boom (kart
  4.4 → 6.2), a 2.4 m pivot (kart 1.5) and FOV 66° → 76° (+6° boosting).
- The camera rig's vehicle mode uses those numbers unchanged: **no `src/client/camera/**` change is needed.**

## The A2 hook: chapter 6's `reach` with `vehicle: true`
A pilot's own `pos` **is** the seat, moved with the plane every tick, so a reach trigger reads the character's
position as usual. It only needs to know the character is in a vehicle (and which kind). Import from
`src/sim/vehicles`:
```ts
import { riderVehicle, vehicleKindOf } from '../vehicles';
// inside the reach check, per squad member c (after the radius test on c.pos):
if (params.vehicle) {
  const v = riderVehicle(sim, c);                 // the kart/plane c is seated in, or null
  if (!v) continue;                               // chapter 6 wants the plane: || vehicleKindOf(v) !== 'plane'
}
if (params.airborne) {
  const v = riderVehicle(sim, c);
  const inAir = v?.plane ? !v.plane.grounded : !c.char!.grounded; // flying pilots and gliding/jumping pets
  if (!inAir) continue;
}
```
Also available: `isFlyingPlane(sim, c)` and `EFlag.Mounted` on the character.

**Critique and the iterations they drove:**
1. **The pilot floated 0.3 m above the saddle.** The Drive pose sits its rump about 0.3 m above its feet, and vehicle
   seats are *feet* positions. I lowered the seat to straddle height and added a handlebar where the pose's paws reach.
2. **The plane sat right under the crosshair.** I raised the camera and aim pivot from 2.0 to 2.4 m (the gun's gimbal
   converges on the same ray) and shortened the boom a little.
3. **Triangles.** The first build was 4.8k triangles; with fewer rounded parts it is 3.46k.

**Weaker spots:**
- At 8 m the plane is small on screen. It's readable from its silhouette and team colors, but a human should judge the
  boom.
- The windsock reads as an orange blob from far away.
- The paw print on the pad hides under the plane.
- **Fun is not human-certified.** The numbers were tuned on the lab and the tests.
