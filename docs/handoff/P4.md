# P4 handoff: the readability + grade pass (Wave 9, look-3)

**Status:** done; nothing is staged or committed (the lead integrates). Every number below comes from a clean snapshot:
`git archive` of HEAD, plus only the P4 files (§9), served by my own Vite on :5305, 1280×720 SwiftShader (WebGL2).
- **Before** is `2cf62b9` (HEAD at hand-back: `13dc2ee` + W9 L3).
- **After** is `2cf62b9` + P4.
- The first full pass ran on `13dc2ee`, as asked. I re-ran everything on `2cf62b9` when L3 landed, because P4's grade and
  sky changes reach L3's map, The Lot. That re-run found a regression there (loop 6, §7), so the final numbers are
  the `2cf62b9` ones. On `13dc2ee` and `2cf62b9` the before numbers agree: within 0.4 luma on the bookmarks and
  within 1.3 on the bench, where the rain moves (`data/score-h2bench.txt`, `history-13dc2ee/`).
- The final snapshot is `2cf62b9` + the §9 files. The character numbers (§3), the gate and the audit ran on exactly
  that. The world, Lot and perf passes (§2, §4, §6) ran one token earlier, with `STYLE.rim.fogCut` 0.5 instead of 0.6
  (loop 7). That token only changes rim (character) materials. The world and Lot labs draw no character, and perf
  counts draws, so those numbers stand.

What P4 changes. No new real light, no new pass, no new draw; the light rig is still key + rim + hemisphere plus the
tier's floodlights.
1. **A character rim + fill** (`toon({ rim })`, a compiled-in term of the hardened material):
   - a cool Fresnel edge plus a flat fill, applied as *light on the albedo*, so team colours get brighter rather than
     paler;
   - it grows with distance: a thin accent at 8 m, a real lift at 30 m;
   - characters (body, neckwear, weapon) opt in; nothing else does;
   - it runs at every detail level, so the low tier gets the same read.
2. **The dusk lift.** The sky's anti-sun "rim" light becomes the dusk sky fill:
   - `sky.ts` `rimI`: ×2.5 while the sun is low, back to ×1 by mid-afternoon;
   - `rimUp`: near-horizontal while the sun is low, so it lights the faces turned from the sun and only grazes the
     ground;
   - exposure 1.0 → 1.25, vignette 0.62 → 0.5.

   Fog, haze and weathering are untouched.
3. **The storm team signal:**
   - characters shed 60 % of the fog and haze (`STYLE.rim.fogCut`, the material's `setupFog`);
   - the grade keeps saturation on pixels within 26° of the two locked team hues from a lower chroma up
     (`signalHueDeg`, `signalHueChroma`).
4. **Low-tier parity:** every character number is measured on high and low.

**Why loop 3's hue protection was needed.** In storm, three things strip crimson at 35 m:
- fog, about 52 % at that range;
- the weather's grade saturation ×0.52;
- the cool light.

The grade's chroma protection (`signalChroma` 0.2–0.42) only keeps pixels that are still strongly coloured. The new
rule keeps pixels within 26° of a team hue from chroma 0.05 up. Only the two locked team hues get it, so the storm
stays grey elsewhere.

**Why the rim colour stays 0xa9c0e6** (loops 4, 5, 7). A cool rim lights blue trims more than crimson, so its colour
trades the corgi read against the cat read. Across all 16 bench cells, 0xa9c0e6 has the best worst case. The fog cut
lifted the storm cats without costing the corgis anything.

- **Snapshot:** `git archive <HEAD> | tar -x -C <dir>`, copy the §9 files in, symlink `node_modules`, and use a Vite
  config with its own `cacheDir`, port 5305 and `hmr: false`.
- **Scripts** (all in `artifacts/p4/tools/`; each is resumable and skips results that exist):
  - `measure2.sh <snap> <label> bench`: bookmarks, other times of day, lineup, bench, perf;
  - `measure-lot.sh`: The Lot;
  - `closeup.sh`: the 6 m and 15 m close-ups;
  - `score2.sh <label>`: scores a pass against the relabelled before masks;
  - `sweep3.sh`, `sweep4.sh`, `sweep5.sh`: the loop-6 and loop-7 sweeps. They need scratch-only URL knobs in the labs,
    which were never shipped; the scripts' headers describe them.
