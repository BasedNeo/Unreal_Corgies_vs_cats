## 1. P2-2: parked karts, and the stuck detours

**Cause (Q4's case, reproduced).** A kart left in the corgis' capture ring closed a gap the nav grid still showed as
open. Two bots pressed into it for 8 and 11 s. (That ring moved with F1's `855ea83`. The fix is general: any kart
parked anywhere.)

**Fix, in three parts.**
- **`src/sim/vehicles/parked.ts` (new).** A kart with no rider, grounded and under 0.3 m/s for 60 ticks, registers its
  footprint as this sim's nav fixture (`setNavFixture`, the same mechanism the kiosks use). The footprint is a box of
  the kart's radius and half height. The fixture is cleared when the kart:
  - moves more than 0.4 m off its spot, or starts moving;
  - is mounted, destroyed or despawned;
  - is cleared by a match restart.

  The grid changes only on a park or an unpark: a region relabel costs 2 ms on the West Yard and 5 ms on the Lot, a
  few times a match. The per-tick cost is one check per kart. Karts driven by bots follow the same grid (`drive.ts`),
  so they route round parked karts too.
- **`src/sim/ai/index.ts`: a latent bug.** The AI context cached the grid at tick 1. A sim's first fixture after tick 1
  (the first parked kart, or any fixture added later) was invisible to every bot. The grid is now re-taken whenever
  `navFixtureVersion(sim)` changes.
- **`src/sim/ai/base-assault-ai.ts`.** Q4's second option as well: a kart trip whose goal lies within 12 m of a stand
  or a capture ring ends 14 m short of it (`BASE_KEEP`, `TRIP_SHORT`), so bots stop leaving karts in rings. Ring and
  guard spots that a parked kart covers are re-picked (`ringSpot`, `zoneSpot` check `walkableAt`).

**The last stuck case: the Lot's scaffold brace (`src/sim/ai/brain.ts`).** The first final lanes run still showed a
cat pinned for 6.5 s on the heap top at (15.3, 96).
- *The trap.* The scaffold's facade brace (`lot/props.ts`, a rolled box) dips from deck height to the heap along the
  edge of a one-cell walkway. The other side of the walkway is a steep drop, closed on the grid.
- *Why the grid misses it.* The cell probe at the cell centre clears the brace by 0.13 m. A pet walking the row's
  edge (a straight path line put it there) presses its head into the brace's underside. The character controller
  cannot slide along an underside: it is a ceiling.
- *Why the old recovery failed.* The random detour at 1.5 s picked cells along the same row and walked back into the
  brace. The spot is not new: Q4's 4.5 s at HEAD was at (15.3, 96), and so were my HEAD lanes run's 4 s. More canyon-lane walkers made it worse.
- *The fix, in two parts:*
  - When a stuck bot's body overlaps a closed cell (8 points 0.4 m around it), it first steps 0.5 m straight away
    from the closed side, then re-plans from there (`unhug`, at most once per 5 s per bot, so a repeat takes the
    random detour).
  - Random detour candidates must be reachable by the body's width as well: the centre line plus both sides 0.3 m
    off it (`bodyLineWalkable`). The old centre-line candidate stays as the fallback.
- *Result on that seed:* stuck 1.5 s, then through.

## 2. P2-3: bot throws that land (`src/sim/ai/ordnance-ai.ts`, the X4 block in `brain.ts`)

**Cause.** A grenade flies up to 1.7 s and blows 2.2 s after the release. X4 threw at clusters wherever they stood, and
a sprinting pet is 20 m away by the blast. Q4's metric (a blast *hits* when an enemy of the thrower takes a hit from it
on the blast tick) gave Base Assault 1 hit in 35 blasts. In TDM, bots rarely bunch, so throws hardly happened
(0.7–1.0 a match).

**Rules now, and why each exists:**
1. **Only holding targets.** A pet *holds* when it has stayed within 2.5 m of one spot for 1 s. `noteBodies` is fed
   every tick by the AI system and keeps the spot and the running mean of where the pet has been. Every counted
   member of a cluster must hold. Holding pets include a pet strafing in a firefight, a guard, a carrier waiting in
   its ring and a sniper.
2. **A lone camper counts:** one pet holding 1 s with ≥ 60 % health.
   - Why: X4's own role statement ("flush clusters and campers"), and TDM needs it, because TDM bots rarely bunch.
   - Why the health floor: most single-target misses were pets that died to the guns before the fuse. A grenade is
     an opener.
   - Measured, a 2 × 2 sweep of health floor (0.45 / 0.6) × hold time (1 / 1.5 s): 0.6 / 1 s gave the most throws
     at the best hit rate.
   - This changes one premise of X4's test (`tests/unit/ordnance-ai.test.ts`). It used to say "a lone enemy never";
     it now says "a lone enemy only once it has camped `singleTicks`" (§5).
3. **Aim and coverage use the pet's mean spot**, not its position this tick: a strafing pet swings round its spot.
4. **Feet planted through the wind-up** (`brain.ts`: `mv` is zeroed while the intent is active). The arc is solved
   from where the bot stands, and a sprint covers 3 m or more in a wind-up.
5. **The release re-plans from where the thrower stands.**
   - If the target moved off, it keeps winding up to `windMax`, checking every 6 ticks for a fresh plan.
   - Otherwise it re-solves the old plan's aim point from its current position (a release can't be cancelled).
   - Test: a thrower still sidestepping when the wind-up starts lands within 1.5 m of the plan.
6. **Nobody walks into their own live grenade** (`keepClearOfOwnBlast`): the inward move component is dropped for the
   fuse, and a bot inside the blast radius + 1.5 m steps out. Q4 saw storming attackers sprint into their own blast.
7. **TDM only: a grenadier closes in on a camper** (`throwApproachBand`, used as the brain's engage band like the
   Assault's bark push).
   - When: a healthy grenadier (≥ 50 % hp) fighting a holding, healthy target 22–36 m away. That is beyond a lob,
     where most TDM firefights are.
   - What: it closes to 12–19 m for up to 4 s, at most once every 8 s.
   - Not in objective modes: there the objective owns where a bot stands (guards hold their zone, chasers hunt the
     carrier). On 5 West Yard BA seeds, 15 captures with it off vs 13 on (noise-level; the design reason decides).
8. **Never mid-climb** (a nav link). Carriers still never throw (G4b's rule, unchanged).

The cluster gate (≥ 2 within 4 m) is not loosened: the lone camper is its own rule, and a moving pair is still no
target. The damage band and gun TTK are untouched: nothing in `src/sim/combat/**` or `src/shared/**` changed.

**Tests** (`tests/unit/ai-ordnance-bots.test.ts`):

## 3. P2-4: Base Assault pushes use the Lot's lanes (`src/sim/ai/base-assault-ai.ts`)

- **Push cycles.** Each team push (rally, then storm) has a cycle number. A new cycle starts after every storm and
  every steal.
- **Lane pick.** Each cycle picks a lane with L3's weighted `pickLane`, keyed by (world seed, 7919 + team, cycle). It
  is deterministic and needs no rng.
- **Walking the lane.** The push's attackers walk the lane's waypoints in their team's direction: corgis forward,
  cats backward, as in `lanes.ts`.
  - A bot joining a push mid-way starts at the waypoint nearest it, or the next one if it is already past that.
  - A waypoint is reached within 6 m.
  - Healthy attackers rush the way (a *ball run*), as G4b's attackers do.
- **Rally.** The squad gathers at the lane's enemy-side end instead of G4b's A* rally, then storms as before. Every
  lane end on the Lot is 20–60 m from the enemy stand (tested); an end outside that falls back to the A* rally.
- **Who stays off the lanes.** Carriers, escorts, chasers and returners keep the shortest routes: a lane is for the
  approach, not the run home.
- **The West Yard** has no lanes: unchanged.
