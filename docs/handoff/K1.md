# K1 handoff: character polish (Wave 3)

**Status:** done. Class silhouettes now read at 30–40 m, and combat faces read determined, scrappy and
smug instead of startled. Face close-ups at the hero tier are smoother. Every kit stays in budget.

The `Avatar` contract is unchanged. One lead-side line is needed to get the kill grin in game (§Integration 1).

**Biggest finding: the ink hull swamps distant characters.** It is not in my paths (§Integration 2). The outline pass keeps
the ink at ~1.3 px (at 720p) at every distance, and every primitive in the merged body mesh gets its own ring. At 35 m
a character is ~12×30 px, so it becomes a near-solid ink blob and only its outer silhouette survives. See
`k1-before-lineup-35m-x3.png`. My kits are designed for that reality: each class breaks the body outline in its own
direction, so the silhouette alone carries the class. A lab experiment (`?inkfar=8`) shows how much colour a
distance-capped ink width would give back on top of that.

## 2. Attitude: combat faces

**The root cause of "cute, not fierce" was geometric.** The iris, pupil and highlight stuck out past the upper-lid shell:
- lid radius 1.08 r;
- iris front 1.1 r, iris rim 1.12 r;
- highlight 1.17 r.

So `furious` lowered the lids *behind* a still-round, startled eye. The fix:
- Iris, pupil and highlight are now spherical caps on the eyeball (≤ 1.05 r), each with its pole on its own axis. This also removes the 6–8-sided "hexagon eyes": the old pole-up discs drew their front outline from only a few rows.
- Cat slit pupils are squeezed, then re-projected onto the sphere.
- Lid shells are 1.1 r.
- A test asserts that the lid shells enclose every iris/pupil/highlight vertex on both species and both tiers.

**New face channels** (`face.ts` `FaceParams`):
- `browIn` (brows knit toward the nose);
- `smile` / `smirk` / `snarl` (mouth corners);
- `chin` (chin-down glare, with the eyes compensating to stay on target).

Brows are now wedges, thick at the inner end, so an angle reads as a V. Lowered brows also move *forward*; before, they sank into the cranium and behind the lids.

**Mouth:**
- An ink lip line (+ philtrum) on the corgi muzzle / cat whisker pads. Its ends are skinned to two new mouth-corner bones (`mouth.L`, `mouth.R`; skeleton 45 → 47 of 48).
- A lower tooth row on the chin, revealed as the jaw drops. The corgi chin was lowered 1–1.5 cm, so small jaw angles now show a gap: before, the chin sat almost entirely inside the muzzle and `jaw 0.4` barely showed.
- Upper fangs: two small ones for cats, one snaggle fang for corgis on the smirk side. Their tips peek under the lip.

**Moods** (`FaceController.choose`), in priority order:

| Priority | Condition | Face |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Emote | Happy |
| 2 | **Kill** (new `trigger('kill')`) | **smug grin** for 1.8 s. It pre-empts the firing face so it reads mid-fight. Adds a head toss and an ear flick |
| 3 | Hit | **grit** for 0.55 s: squeezed eyes, bared teeth |
| 4 | Firing | **furious**: brows down and in, glare, snarl with open jaw |
| 5 | Aiming | **determined**: narrowed level glare, chin down, mouth set |
| 6 | Low HP, falling | Terrified |
| 7 | Otherwise | Idle mood |

- Cats narrow to slit pupils in combat.
- The neutral face is a touch more confident: slightly lowered lids and a half-smile.
- The result is attitude, not horror: no gore, no fangs longer than 2 cm.

## 3. Faceted faces in close-ups

Three causes, three fixes:
1. **Fur tufts shaded as facets.**
   - Tufts displaced every other vertex *and* the normals came from the tufted surface, so the cheeks, ruff, butt buns, tail and pants shaded as zig-zag facets.
   - Worse, with an odd segment count the seam column (u = 1 duplicates u = 0) got a different tuft, which opened a crack on the *front* of the cheeks and ruff.
   - Fix: `ellipsoid`/`sweep` take a `tuft` multiplier that shapes positions only. Normals come from the untufted shape (`Prim.nrmPos`), and `tuftCol()` is seam-safe. Both tiers benefit at zero triangle cost.
2. **Seams between intersecting blobs.** Cheeks and cat pads use a *normal transfer*: `MeshBuilder.add(…, { proxy })` blends their normals 55% / 35% toward a smooth ellipsoid around the cranium. The toon bands then flow across the cheek-to-head junction; positions and ink are unchanged. Both tiers benefit.
3. **Resolution at the hero tier.**
   - Face parts and headgear use a separate multiplier: `FACE_DETAIL.hero = 1.12`.
     - Cranium 22×16; cheeks 13×9; muzzle 13 around.
     - Nose, lids and irises are also denser.
     - Helmet domes 16×6.
   - The body drops from `DETAIL.hero` 0.93 to 0.86. Most small segment counts round to the same values.
   - The NPC tier keeps 0.66.

Other savings used to pay for the above:
- The fur torso stops 12 cm inside the closed vest shell.
- The double chest plate became one plate with a painted trim.
- The sclera is a front-facing shell open at the back.

## Integration notes for the lead
1. **Kill grin (1 line in `main.ts`):**
   ```ts
   if (ev.e === 'death' && ev.by !== ev.id && ev.by >= 0) views.trigger(ev.by, 'kill')
   ```
   `trigger` is a string, so the `Avatar` contract is unchanged; only its doc comment could list `'kill'`. Without this line the kill grin never shows in game. Aim/fire/hit faces already work from `AvatarFrame` + the existing triggers.
2. **Distance-capped ink (style lane, `src/client/style/style-webgpu.js`, proposal).**
   - Problem: `toonOutlinePass` extrudes by `thickness × pos.w`, i.e. constant screen width at every distance.
   - Proposal: cap the `pos.w` factor at ~8 m, so beyond that the ink keeps a constant world width and thins with distance, like a comic's atmospheric linework:
     ```js
     … .mul(min(pos.w, float(8)))
     ```
   - The lab does exactly this by patching `ToonOutlinePassNode.prototype._createMaterial` under `?inkfar=8`.
   - Compare `k1-*-lineup-35m.png` with `*-inkfar8.png`: fur, team colours and gear colours come back at 35 m.
   - It affects every toon asset, not just characters, so it needs a look at the world too. Characters read at range without it; it is a bonus.
3. **Warden cone** at hip-fire sits ~2° left of the crosshair from the local camera (shoulder 0.55 m, boom 4.2 m); at ADS it is ~8° away. It never covers the crosshair, but a playtest should confirm that it does not distract.
4. The Overwatch **beacon** makes snipers visible at range by design (a red dot above the head). If that is too strong a tell, drop `glow()` intensity in `index.ts` (2.2) or remove the beacon ellipsoid from `kitGlow`.

## Known issues / next
- Hero budget margin is thin: corgi overwatch 5,970 and corgi breacher 5,962 of 6,000. Any new hero-tier gear needs a trim elsewhere.
- In-game faces are what *others* see, i.e. the NPC tier (`FACE_DETAIL.npc` 0.66). The tuft and proxy-normal fixes apply there. The extra face segments are hero-only (lab, menus, future kill-cam), because the NPC budget has ~70 triangles of headroom.
- The mouth line is a thin ink tube: crisp in portraits, sub-pixel at gameplay distance (by design; eyes and brows carry the expression there). The one-sided smirk depends on view side: it lifts the character's left corner.
- The hit flinch still throws the head back hard for ~0.25 s (L1 spring). The gritted face reads best just after the peak.
- The Infiltrator hood peak always flops to the character's left; a seeded side would add variety, but the class read would then need a mirrored silhouette check.
- The silhouette metric scores the idle pose only. Sprint (corgi zoomies) folds everything; masts and wings still stick out, but no test covers it.
