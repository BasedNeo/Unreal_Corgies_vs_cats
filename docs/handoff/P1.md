## 1. P1: sim tick budget

### 1.1 Where the time goes (baseline, measured)
- **Profile.** `node --cpu-prof` on a headless 28-bot room: `KinematicCharacterController.computeColliderMovement` is 46 % of all process time.
- **Cost per call:**

  | Surface | µs per call |
  |---|---|
  | Undulating 1 m lawn | 80–120 |
  | Coplanar 1 m cells | 42 |
  | 2-triangle plane | 27 |
  | Cuboid | 13 |
  | In the air | 1.2 |

- **What each moving call does.** I read the Rapier 0.36 / parry 0.31 source (the versions compiled into `rapier3d-compat` 0.21). Each moving call runs:
  - a grounded check: contact manifolds against every triangle in the loosened capsule AABB, 8–18 triangles at 1 m cells, with a fresh workspace each time;
  - per iteration, a GJK shape cast and another grounded check;
  - a snap cast.
- **Consequence.** The cost scales with the non-coplanar triangles in reach, not with hits: 0-hit calls still cost 57 µs.
- **Systems** (baseline, best-of-3, 28 bots): movement 2.0 · ai 0.25–0.35 · physics-step 0.2 · others ≈ 0.15 ms. In `qa-budget`, the room overhead (snapshot encoding for 12 clients) adds ≈ 0.45 ms.

### 1.2 Levers measured (28 characters, same-process interleaved A/B unless noted)

### 1.3 What changed: the terrain fast path (`src/sim/world/build.ts`, my lane)
- **Which moves.** 90–92 % of movement calls in a full room are plain grounded moves over open terrain: no prop, kiosk, kart or barrier within 0.3 m, and a gentle slope. For exactly those, `terrainFastMove()` returns the controller's result analytically.
- **Geometry.** It uses the same grid triangles the collider is built from. `sphereRestOnGrid()` gives the exact height at which the capsule's bottom sphere (radius r + the 2 cm offset) rests on the faces, edges and vertices. It matches Rapier's own ball casts to p99 0.06 mm; the ~1 mm outliers are Rapier's GJK, not the analytic side.
- **Motion.** It replays the controller's rules:
  - travel along the input until the offset gap closes;
  - Rapier's `handle_slopes()` decomposition (wall and non-slip cases) plus the 1e-4 normal nudge;
  - snap-to-ground.
- **Settings.** It reads them from the live controller (`offset()`, `normalNudgeFactor()`, `snapToGroundDistance()`, slope angles), so it follows any change in `sim.ts`.
- **Fallback.** Anything else returns `false` and the real controller runs:
  - any collider the controller would see within reach. It uses the same broad phase and the same `CHARACTER_MOVE_FILTER` group test, so props, kiosks, karts, C2 barriers and colliders created this tick are seen exactly as the controller sees them;
  - supports steeper than 30°;
  - not resting on the terrain (gap outside −2 mm … +5 cm);
  - moving up (jumps, pads, upward knockback);
  - ground-pounding;
  - off the grid;
  - worlds without a terrain grid (flat test worlds).
- **Parity with prediction.** `buildStaticWorld()` registers the terrain grid per Rapier `World`. The authority and the client predictor both build through it, so both take the same path. Prediction stays exact: `net-prediction` and `net-matrix` are green with the edit.
- **Fidelity against the real controller on identical inputs.** Shadow mode, 28-bot room, 38k eligible calls:
  - horizontal difference: p50 0.001 mm, p99 0.4 mm;
  - vertical difference: p50 0.008 mm, p99 5 mm;
  - grounded: agrees in all but 1 of ~49k calls.
- **The larger differences are controller artifacts that the fast path does not have.** Every one I logged fell into two kinds:
  - the controller hops 1–2 cm above the lawn at triangle edges and falls back the next tick;
  - it sometimes dead-stops for one tick on open lawn (`desired x -0.145 -> kcc -0.002`), the same artifact QA saw on the flat slab.
- **A/B switch.** `terrainFastPath.enabled` (the perf tools take `--fast 0|1`).

### 1.4 Proof
- **New unit tests.** `tests/unit/perf-terrain-fast.test.ts`, 5 tests:
  - sphere-rest height vs Rapier ball casts;
  - fast move vs the real controller from the same start: 40 open-lawn spots × 16 directions × walk/run/sprint; horizontal p99 < 1 mm, vertical p90 < 1 mm, always grounded, > 95 % eligible;
  - every fallback case;
  - flat worlds keep the controller;

## 2. P2: a low tier that is really cheaper

### 2.1 Why low was only 4–6 % cheaper
`tools/perf-render.mjs` (new) counts GL draws and triangles per frame and splits them by pass. At the same deterministic view (free mode, spawn, `t=0.45`, 1280×720), high was:
- **shadow pass:** 20 draws / 238k tris;
- **scene:** 88 draws / 956k tris. Of that, prop crease-ink lines are 159k tris, and the toon outline pass draws every toon mesh a second time;
- **fullscreen:** 13 draws, 12 of them bloom.

The old low tier only removed scatter foliage (72k tris). The engine profile in `engine/quality.ts` (pixel ratio, bloom, draw distance) was applied nowhere, as U1's `quality-note.ts` had already noted.

### 2.2 What changed (my lane: `src/client/engine/**`)
- **`quality.ts`: tiers as data, in two groups.**
  - Engine knobs, applied live: pixel-ratio bounds, the shadow pass, bloom. Outlines stay on in every tier.
  - World knobs, read by the world view: shadow-map size, foliage, garden density, prop creases, clouds, terrain detail, rain capacity. Garden density and prop creases switch live.
  - Adds `toQualityTier()`, which accepts both the 'medium' and 'med' spellings.
  - I dropped `drawDistance`. The sky dome is a 1500 m sphere, so a far-plane cut would clip the sky; it needs world-lane work.
- **`renderer.ts`:** `createRenderContext(app, { forceWebGL, quality })`, plus `ctx.setQuality(tier)` and `ctx.adaptive`. `applyEngineQuality()` does three things:
  - flips `renderer.shadowMap.enabled`. That flag is part of three's render-object cache key, so flipping it live is safe;
  - swaps the pipeline's output graph between "scene + bloom" and "scene only". A bloom node that is no longer referenced is never rendered;
  - sets the adaptive-resolution bounds.
- **`adaptive-quality.ts`:** `setBounds(min, max)` clamps the ratio and applies it immediately.

| Tier | Pixel ratio | Shadows | Bloom | Foliage | Garden tall grass | Prop creases | Clouds / terrain detail |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 0.5–0.85 | off | off | 0 | 45 % | off | off |
| medium | 0.6–1.15 | on (1536) | on | 0.6 | 75 % | on | on |
| high | 0.75–1.5 | on (2048) | on | 1 | 100 % | on | on |

## 6. Known issues
- **Full suite on the real tree** (my files only, fast path not yet wired): `npx vitest run` → 368/370 at load 14. Neither failure comes from these files:
  - `world-garden` "tall grass": the in-flight C2/D3 failure described in §1.4;
  - `vehicles-yard` "16 bots + a kart driver for 30 s": it timed out at 33.8 s under load, and passes alone (6/6).
- **Timing noise.** The box has 4 cores shared by 5 agents; load was 6–17 during this work.
  - Every comparison here is alternated or interleaved and best-of-N.
  - Single-run wall p95 from any tool is 2–3× inflated at load > 8.
  - At low load the fast-path tick should come out lower still.
- **The fast path changes behaviour slightly (documented, not zero).** On open terrain, characters follow the lawn exactly at the controller's 2 cm offset, instead of the controller's own ±1–2 cm edge hops and rare one-tick stops.
  - It never takes over while a character sits inside the offset shell (gap < −2 mm). The controller leaves characters spawned on slopes there.
  - Those characters stay on the controller until it moves them out.
- **Tail latency.** Tick max is still 5–7 ms, from AI and combat bursts. The budget is on p95.
- **Rapier ray precision (pre-existing, not touched).** Straight-down rays within ~1e-14 of a grid line (x = −0.00…) miss the full 1 m trimesh: 4 797 misses on a 0.1 m lattice. The tests' 0.5 m lattice has 0. `WorldData.height()` remains the right ground probe.
- **Draw count growth.** TDM at high is now 258 draws (Q1 measured 182–190); new S1/C2 content added draws. That is still inside the 400 budget.
- **Tier not exposed for tests.** `debug-hook` does not expose the tier or the pixel ratio; `perf-render` reports them as null and takes the tier from the URL.
