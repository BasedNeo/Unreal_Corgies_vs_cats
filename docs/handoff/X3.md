## 2. Weapon models (`weapons.ts`)
**Reference:** the mood board's battered rifle — a chunky receiver, a long mag, a rail and an optic, dark metal with
worn edges.

**Construction:**
- **Parts:** each weapon is built from hard-surface parts:
  - `hexa` / `box` / `slab`: 12 triangles each;
  - flat-capped `prism`s: 20–36 triangles;
  - straps: open 4-sided sweeps.
- **Why it's cheaper:** the old superellipsoid box cost 30+ triangles.
- **Faces:** each face has its own vertices, so it takes its own tone:
  - lit tops;
  - grimy undersides;
  - chipped corners, seeded by position.
- **Normals and ink:** normals are still welded, so the ink hull is intact, and hard edges come back as crease ink.

**Colour:**
- Every colour is a palette token or a mix of two.
- S4's HARDENED tokens are used when present: `hazardOchre`, `oxblood`, `charcoal`, `brass`, `gunmetal`, `steel`,
  `canvas`.

**Factions:**
- **Corgis:** gunmetal + hazard-ochre furniture.
- **Cats:** charcoal + oxblood + brass.
- **Team signal:** tape bands in the team colours (blue/gold, crimson/black).
- **Charm:** a dog tag (corgis) or a collar bell (cats) on the sling swivel.

Budget:
- Every weapon stays under its pre-X3 triangle count (`WEAPON_TRI_BUDGET`, asserted in `weapon-view.test.ts`).
- The character kit budgets (6,000 / 3,500, shared with K2's armour) therefore get cheaper, not dearer.
- Draw calls per weapon are unchanged: mesh + crease ink + glow.

**Contract kept:**
- `buildWeapon(cls, team, q)` is unchanged.
- The origin is the right-hand grip; the barrel runs along −Z.
- `leftGrip` and `muzzle` are recomputed per gun. The test checks the muzzle is the front-most point and the grip lies
  on the gun.
- `boss/sniper.ts` builds the Overwatch kit as before.

**Material:**
- `WeaponGeo.finish = { metal, wear, grime }` and `geometry.userData.weaponFinish`. K2's `index.ts` already feeds it into
  `toon({ surface: 'weapon', ...finish })`.
- I lowered `metal` to 0.2–0.45. At 0.85 the ochre paint rendered almost black, because the S4 material scales diffuse
  by (1 − metal) (screenshot `fire-rifle-side-v3`).
- The geometry also carries a per-part `surface` attribute:

  | Part | rough | metal | grime | wear |

  | polymer | 0.78 | 0 | 0.35 | 0.25 |
  | canvas | 0.92 | 0 | 0.5 | 0.1 |

  It is written through `MeshBuilder.surface()`. It is only used once the weapon material reads it (§6.4).

## 3. Firing (`presets-weapons.ts`, `weapon-fx.ts`, `light-pulses.ts`, director `fire`)
Everything starts inside `fx.onGameEvent('fire')`, the same bus callback that plays the report:
- **Muzzle flash by weight class:**
  - a white-hot core;
  - a coloured burst;
  - a forward flame tongue (the Petal shape, stretched along the shot) with an inner white tongue;
  - brake/nozzle petals: rifle 2 sideways, sprinkler 3 in a Y, longshot 4 in a cross;
  - thin sparks;
  - halftone smoke wisps that drift forward, rise, grow and thin out (a comic screen-door, not a new transparent pass).
- **Sizes:** flash radius and tongue length are pistol 0.15 / 0.10 (suppressed), rifle 0.28 / 0.34, longshot
  0.42 / 0.55, mortar 0.55 / 0.32.
- **Frisbee launcher:** an air-blast ring and speed lines (flywheels, not powder).
- **Light pulse:**
  - a fixed pool of point lights: 2 on high, 1 on medium, 0 on low;
  - a 12 ms attack, then a quadratic fall over 60–90 ms;
  - the dimmest light is stolen, and the local player's shots are weighted so your own gun always lights your corner;
  - plus a soft additive ground spill (the Glow shape, quadratic falloff) under the muzzle;
  - idle lights stay in the scene at intensity 0, because adding or removing lights recompiles every lit material;
  - peaks: rifle 2.4, pistol 1.2, longshot 4.5 (red), mortar 4, explosions 7–14.
- **Tracers:** amber halo + white-hot core streaks at 190 m/s, head-anchored so they die exactly at the hit point.
  - Rifle and pistol get tracers.
  - The longshot keeps its full laser beam.
  - The sprinkler keeps its inked droplet spray.
- **Casings:**
  - rifle brass, short pistol brass, long magnum brass, red shotgun hulls;
  - they leave the ejection port (the weapon's `ejectBack` behind the muzzle), kicked right, up and back, spinning;
  - they bounce on `heightAt` and shrink away after ~1.5 s;
  - off on the low tier.
- **Sync:** particles spawned between frames skip their first update (`freshFirstFrame`). The first rendered frame
  after the event therefore shows the flash at full strength, on the same frame the report starts.

## 4. Impacts, marks, pet hits, explosions
- **What was hit** (`SurfaceMap.classify`), from WorldData alone:
  - water zones;
  - prop boxes (rotated, face normal) and cylinders (side/top), with the material from the prop `type` by keyword;
  - X1 destructibles by kind (never marked: they break);
  - terrain (height-gradient normal; grass / dirt / sand from `surface()`).

  Details:
  - `boundary` blockers are ignored.
  - Anything else (karts, drones, barriers) gets generic chips and no mark.
  - It uses a uniform 4 m XZ grid built once. It allocates nothing (200,000 West Yard queries measured).
  - E4's new props read sensibly with no table edit: `sandbag_*` → soft, `*container*` → metal, `*feeder*`/`*tower*`
    → wood, `picket_barricade` → wood.
- **Impact FX by surface:**

  | Surface | Effect |
  |---|---|
  | dirt / grass / sand | bouncing clods, a soil jet, a halftone dust puff (+ grass bits) |
  | metal | a white flash, a fan of reflected sparks that bounce, dark shards, a wisp |
  | wood | pale splinters (the Shard shape), sawdust |
  | stone | chips + a couple of pale sparks + dust |
  | water | a splash column, a crown ring on the surface, mist |
  | soft | fibres + dust |

  The spray direction is the shot reflected about the normal, leaned onto the normal.
- **Impacts wait for their tracer:** `impactDelay` = distance / 190 m/s, capped at 0.3 s; spray arcs up to 0.6 s. A
  fixed 48-slot ring holds pending impacts. The audio impact takes the same delay on the audio clock.
- **Buckshot:** the authority reports only the sprinkler's first pellet. The client scatters 3 small extra impacts in
  the spread cone on the same surface. This is presentation only, flagged in the code and in §7.
- **Marks** (`decal-pool.ts`, `decal-mesh.ts`):
  - one draw call for all marks;
  - the ring is capped at 64 / 40 / 16 (high / medium / low);
  - a new mark overwrites the oldest, and the 4 oldest of a full ring are already fading, so recycling never pops;
  - bullet holes fade after 24 s, scorch after 40 s.
  - **Bullet holes:** an inked core with a rim that tells the surface: bright dented steel, pale splintered wood, chipped
    stone, a soft dark pock in soil.
  - **Explosion scorch:** soot with a ragged edge, a brown halo and stipple.
  - Marks are depth-tested with a polygon offset and lifted 12 mm off the surface.
- **Pets (rated for everyone):**
  - L5's fur tufts and team fleck stay.
  - A small red comic splat pops and shrinks in 0.25 s on the viewer's side of the pet, with two droplets (bigger on a
    crit). It is never a pool and never on the ground.
  - **THWACK!** (new word) on body hits ≥ 10 damage, per victim with a 1.4 s cooldown and a 40 % chance (kept sparse: a 3v3 bot fight already fills the sky with words).
  - SPLAT!/THWACK! at ≥ 25 damage; POW!/BONK! on crits.
  - Kittens' claw swipes get three pale slash streaks.
- **Explosions:** on top of L5's blast:
  - a rolling fireball of glow petals and hot inked puffs;
  - a dark halftone smoke column;
  - embers;
  - a dirt fountain;
  - a big light pulse and ground spill;
  - a scorch on whatever it went off against (ground or wall).

## 5. Hit confirmation, sound, camera
**Hitmarker** (`ui/hit-feedback.ts`), four inked diagonal ticks over the crosshair:

| Kind | Look | Life |
|---|---|---|
| hit | white, pop 1.35 → 1 | 0.24 s |
| crit | gold, bigger | 0.32 s |
| kill | red, spins in over 160 ms, plus a red ring burst | 0.6 s |

- Rapid hits stack: a boost grows and lengthens the ticks through a landed burst.
- A plain hit never downgrades a crit or kill that is still showing.
- The model is pure and tested. The DOM view writes only changed style values.

**Sound** (`audio/presets-weapons.ts`): each report is layered:
- a noise crack;
- a sine thump;
- a band-passed blast;
- a filtered tail;
- the gun's mechanism: bolt, slide, pump rack, bolt cycle.

Plus:
- impacts by surface, texture only: within 26 m, at most one per 45 ms, delayed to the visual impact;
- `hitThud` for the shooter (UI bus): hit / crit + tink / kill double thump;
- brass tinkles, for your own gun only.

The old toy voices (`squeakPew`, `snap`, …) stay exported.

Offline render: Chromium `OfflineAudioContext`, 48 kHz, at play gains, no master compressor. The level is the plain
max 100 ms RMS.

What this shows:
- The rifle now sits level with a thwack: 9 dB over the old squeak, 12 dB under an explosion.
- The heavies sit 3–5 dB over the rifle; the suppressed pistol sits 3 dB under it.
- The new voices peak at 0.72 at most (the old snap, thwack and boom peak at 0.95–1.39 into the master limiter).

**Camera** (`third-person.ts`):
- `kick(pitch, yaw, recover)` drives the rendered view pitch/yaw on an exact critically damped spring
  (frame-rate independent).
  - The sizes are the weapon's `RecoilDef` × `viewKick` (0.5–0.6). For example the rifle kicks 0.28° per shot and
    settles in 0.12 s; the longshot kicks 1.8°.
  - It is capped at 2.9° / 1.1°, so a long burst never walks the view.
  - It is 35 % lighter when aiming.
- `punch(deg)`: FOV −3.2° on a local kill, −1.2° on a crit, back in ~0.28 s.
- Both scale with the Shake setting; 0 turns them off.
- They are applied after `lookAt` with `rotateX`/`rotateY`. Input yaw/pitch, the AIM_RAY pivot and everything the
  client sends are untouched.

## 6. Paste-ready wiring (lead)
Everything above already runs with the current `main.ts`, with one gap: impacts classify against terrain only, because
the FX derives it from `heightAt`. You get ground puffs and ground holes, but no metal, wood or water impacts, and no
marks on props.

The snippets below add props and water, the view kick, the FOV punch and the new hitmarker.

**6.1 `src/client/main.ts`: FX + audio get the world** (next to the existing `createFx` line)
```ts
const fx = createFx(ctx.scene, ctx.camera, views, { heightAt: (x, z) => worldData.height(x, z), world: worldData });
audio.setWorld(worldData);
```
If the world data is rebuilt (a map change), call `fx.setWorld(worldData); audio.setWorld(worldData);` again.

**6.2 `src/client/main.ts`: kick, punch and hitmarker**

Import:
```ts
import { createHitFeedback } from './ui/hit-feedback';
```

After `const hud = createHud(...)`:
```ts
const hitFx = createHitFeedback(ui);
```

In `bus.on('game', (ev) => { ... })`, replace the three FX / camera lines:
```ts
    const r = fx.onGameEvent(ev);
    if (r.shake > 0) cam.shake(r.shake);
    if (r.kickPitch !== 0 || r.kickYaw !== 0) cam.kick(r.kickPitch, r.kickYaw, r.kickRecover); // visual recoil (view only)
    if (r.fovPunch > 0) cam.punch(r.fovPunch);                                                  // kill / crit confirm
    audio.onGameEvent(ev);
    hud.onGameEvent(ev);
    hitFx.onGameEvent(ev, net?.localEntity ?? -1);
```

In the frame loop, right after `hud.update({...})`:
```ts
    hitFx.update();
```

**6.3 HUD: retire the old hit marker, so there is one marker, not two**

Either add this to `src/client/ui/hud-style.ts` (inside the stylesheet string):
```css
#cvc-hud .hm{display:none}
```
or, in `src/client/ui/hud.ts` `onGameEvent`, delete the two calls: `hud.hitMarker(false, ev.crit)` in `case 'hit'` and
`hud.hitMarker(true)` in `case 'death'`.

The crosshair, damage arcs and everything else in the HUD stay as they are.

**6.4 (optional, K2 / lead) per-part weapon surfaces**

In `src/client/procgen/characters/index.ts`, change `weaponMat` to read the per-vertex surface my geometry carries:
```ts
const weaponMat = toon({ color: 0xffffff, vertexColors: true, surfaceAttr: true, surface: 'weapon', ...(finish ?? {}) } as Parameters<typeof toon>[0]);
```
The effect:
- The metal parts become metal (0.85).
- The paint stays paint (0.15) and chips at its edges.
- Grips stay rough polymer.

With `surfaceAttr`, the style factory takes rough, metal, grime and wear from the attribute. The per-weapon `finish`
is then ignored for those four; it stays the fallback for a material without the flag. S4's factory as I read it:
`HardenedToonMaterial` uses `surf.x..w` in place of the material references.

**6.5 Settings:** `fx.setQuality(st.quality)` (already in `applySettings`) now also sets the tier's mark cap
(16 / 40 / 64), brass on/off and the light count (0 / 1 / 2). A tier change rebuilds lit materials once, like the
shadow toggle.

## 7. Gameplay numbers: none changed
Not one of these changed:
- `src/shared/**`;
- fire rate, spread, bloom, damage, falloff, pellets, recoil data, ammo, reload;
- the protocol, the input, the sim.

Recoil kick is visual only. The camera rotates the rendered view after `lookAt`, so the crosshair can sit up to about
0.3–0.4° off the true aim during full-auto rifle fire (about 5 px at 720p). Hits land where the sim says: the tracer
and marks always go to the authoritative hit point.

Two presentation-only liberties:
1. The sprinkler's 3 extra buckshot impacts are scattered in the spread cone around the reported first pellet (the
   authority does not send every pellet).
2. A world impact is drawn when the tracer arrives (≤ 0.3 s), not on the shot frame.

## 9. Known issues and follow-ups
**Wear up close:**
- At 1:1 on a pet-scale gun the wear is only readable up close, in the lit tops, grimy undersides and chipped corners.
- Real chips and scratches need S4's screen-space edge wear, which keys on curvature. My boxes have welded (smooth)
  normals, so that curvature is spread across faces.
- If S4 wants crisper wear on guns, the next step is a bevel strip per box edge (≈ +32 triangles per receiver). There is
  headroom: the rifle has 90 triangles left under its ceiling.

**Lights:**
- Up to 2 extra point lights run per fragment on every lit material (high tier), and S4 is adding sodium practicals.
- If the GPU budget on a mid laptop is tight, drop the FX high tier to 1 light (`FX_TIERS.high.lights`). The ground
  spill sprite keeps most of the read.

**Words:**
- The rifle still pops L5's SQUEAK! word sometimes (35 %, 2.5 s cooldown per shooter).
- If the HARDENED tone wants the rifle wordless, delete its `FIRE_WORDS` entry in `fx/onomatopoeia.ts`.

**Splat:**
- The comic splat is deliberately small and brief (≤ 0.24 m, 0.25–0.3 s): comic, never a pool.
- If the rating owner wants it gone, drop the `W.comicSplat` call in `fx/index.ts` `case 'hit'`.

**Camera trauma:**
- The per-shot camera trauma (`WEAPON_FX.kick`) rose slightly for the heavier guns: rifle 0.035→0.045,
  pistol 0.05→0.06, longshot 0.09→0.12, mortar 0.14→0.16, sprinkler 0.02→0.10.
- hud.ts reads the same number for its crosshair bloom animation, so the HUD crosshair kicks a little more too. This is
  visual only.

**Remote shots:**
- Remote muzzles use the avatar's `muzzleWorld`, which is one frame stale.
- The authority's `fire.x/y/z` is the eye, so a remote tracer can start a few cm off the barrel when the pet turns fast.

**Buckshot:**
- The sprinkler's extra buckshot impacts are presentation only (§7). An authoritative pellet list would need a protocol
  change (lead-owned).

**Surfaces:**
- Karts, drones and barriers classify as generic (no mark). Moving marks would need per-entity decal parenting; out of
  scope.

**Lab:**
- The lab's dusk mood is a stand-in: darker key and hemisphere, slate sky.
- Judge the final colour balance in-game with S4's rig. My FX colours are linearized palette tokens, so they follow the
  grade.

**Sniper boss:**
- `src/client/procgen/boss/sniper.ts` builds the Longshot with `toon({ vertexColors: true, surface: 'weapon' })`, without
  the finish, so its paint renders at the preset's metal 0.6 (darker).
- One-line fix for its owner: add `...asset.weapon.finish` to that `toon()` call (line ≈ 205).
