## 2. Why the corgis' side won

Everything below was measured with a bot harness (`scratchpad/f1/clean/ba.mts`). It reproduces `qa4-ba matches`
match for match: HEAD gives 25 : 12 and 24 : 6 on the same seeds. It also records:
- every carrier death, with its killer's position, role, spawn point and time since spawning;
- every bot stall (wants to move, moves < 1 m/s), by 1 m cell.

**Who kills thieves.** On HEAD, 133 carriers died after robbing the corgi stand.
- Of these, 126 were killed by defenders that had respawned at the two ends of that side's spawn row: 93 at (−64, −70) and 33 at (−6, −79).
- The youngest of these killers was 0.6 s old. The median was 7.6 s in base and 11 s in mirror.
- Thieves robbing the cat stand were killed by defenders from (68, 76) and (74, 64). The youngest was 2 s old; the median was 12–15 s.

**Why the row ends matter.** `Sim.pickSpawn` puts a respawning bot on its team's spawn farthest from the living enemies.
- **Corgi side, before:** the stand sat mid-row. With the thieves at the stand, the farthest corgi spawns were only 27 m and 35 m away. The east one, (−6, −79), was even level with the cat thief's exit.
- **Cat side:** the stand sits at the row's west end. Its farthest spawns are 42–48 m away, behind a corgi thief running south-west.
- **Effect:** thieves from the corgi stand died about twice as fast over their first 40 m. 29 % got past 40 m (16 % with the sides swapped), against 47 % and 59 % from the cat stand.

**Line of sight is not the lever.** Q4's metric correlates with the lean, but it does not drive it.
- My second loop cut the metric's pooled gap from 26.6 to 5.9 points, and the bots leaned harder (82 % / 76 %).
- That wall added a choke point on the route (§4).

**A second, smaller asymmetry: the kart-gate hedgehog.** `hog_kw` at (−24.4, −49.4) sat 1 m from the cat thief's
route home. Its thin beams are missed by the 1 m nav grid (a capsule probe at each cell centre).
- In 20 HEAD matches, carriers stood stuck against it for 35 s in total (1–4 s at a time), and chasers caught them there. Q4's drop-distance data shows the same thing: 9 of 48 drops at 16–18 m.
- Moving the hedgehog alone did not fix the lean: 77 % / 77 % (§4).
- After the flag move, the cat thief's route runs down the east lane and no longer passes it.
- The nav gap itself is outside my lane. See §7.

### 5.2 The qa4 tool fix, and what it means for Q4's cover rows

`coveredWorld()` returned `{ ...base, props }`. The E4 battle layout is registered per WorldData object (`battleOf`,
a WeakMap), so the copy had none, and Base Assault fell back to the spawn-centroid bases.
- The fallback moved both flags and stands. Routes went 161 → 175 m and 169 → 178 m: the "routes move" note in Q4 §2.3.
- So Q4's three `cover` rows (7 : 14, 10 : 16, 7 : 12) measured **relocated bases**, not crates. They are the first hint
  that base placement is the lever.
- The tool now registers the copy: `los --variant cover` keeps the stands (route 161 m).
- `mirroredWorld()` was fine: it sets `WorldData.bases` explicitly.
